Wenxiang Ying Research Publications CV

My research focuses on non-equilibrium quantum dynamics and transport in light–matter hybrid systems, with emphasis on nanophotonic environments, quantum materials, energy transfer, and chiral light–matter interactions.

Methodologically, I employ rigorous and approximate open quantum system approaches—including hierarchical equations of motion, quantum master equations, tensor-network methods, and mixed quantum-classical dynamics—as well as classical electrodynamics simulations (FDTD) to bridge microscopic quantum descriptions with realistic field profiles in nanophotonic and plasmonic environments.

Non-equilibrium Quantum Dynamics in Open Systems

A central theme of my work is the theoretical and computational study of many-body open quantum dynamics, decoherence, and energy transport in complex chemical and material environments. These methods aim to capture non-Markovian environmental effects, quantum coherence, dephasing, and dissipation on equal footing.

  • Hierarchical Equations of Motion (HEOM). Construction and efficient implementation of HEOM for vibrational and electronic degrees of freedom in polaritonic systems, including convergence analysis and interpretation of the hierarchy in terms of environmental memory.
  • Approximate Quantum Master Equations. (Variational) polaron-transformed and perturbative master equations to understand their range of validity in realistic parameter regimes, including analytic rate theories for polariton relaxation dynamics.
  • Tensor Network Methods. Application of tensor-network approaches to capture many-body correlations and quantum entanglement in open systems beyond the reach of exact diagonalization, enabling scalable simulations of large light–matter aggregates.
  • Mixed Quantum-Classical Dynamics. Ehrenfest and surface-hopping methods to describe nonadiabatic processes and to provide intuitive pictures that complement more rigorous approaches, including trajectory-based simulations of polariton relaxation.

Light–Matter Hybrid Systems and Polaritonic Phenomena

A major focus of my doctoral and postdoctoral research is collective strong light-matter coupling and its impact on chemical dynamics, photophysics, and transport. By embedding molecules or materials inside optical cavities or nanophotonic structures (Fabry–Pérot microcavities, plasmonic structures, organic materials, and 2D materials), one can form hybrid light-matter states (polaritons) that modify reaction pathways, energy-transport channels, and emergent many-body phenomena.

  • Vibrational Polariton Chemistry. Recent experiments have demonstrated that chemical kinetics can be enhanced or suppressed by coupling molecular vibrations to quantized radiation modes inside an optical microcavity— a phenomenon known as vibrational strong coupling (VSC) enabled reactivity change. This strategy, if fully understood, offers a paradigm shift in synthetic chemistry through cavity-enabled bond-selective transformations, bypassing intrinsic difficulties such as intramolecular vibrational energy redistribution encountered in mode-selective IR chemistry. I have performed quantum-dynamical studies of how coupling molecular vibrations to cavity modes can enhance, suppress, or reshape reaction rates, including identification of "resonant suppression" conditions and the role of dark-state manifolds.
  • Exciton-Polariton Photophysics and Transport. Microscopic modelling of exciton-photon hybridization in inorganic and organic (molecular) systems. Analysis of how disorder, vibronic coupling, and cavity loss affect exciton and polariton relaxation, decoherence, and propagation. For polariton transport, my research addresses the emergence of ballistic versus diffusive regimes, the possibility of long-range transport under strong coupling, group-velocity renormalization, dispersion engineering, motional narrowing, and the role of quantum coherence.
  • Cavity-Modified Electron Transfer. Fermi's-golden-rule based rate theories for nonadiabatic electron transfer in confined electromagnetic environments, aiming to unify Marcus–Jortner-type descriptions with microscopic cavity QED and to clarify under what conditions cavity modes—including lossy cavities— can substantially alter charge-transfer kinetics.

Electromagnetic Environment Engineering

A complementary strand of my research concerns the engineering and simulation of structured electromagnetic fields in nanophotonic and plasmonic environments, and their impact on molecular and material quantum dynamics.

  • Finite-Difference Time-Domain (FDTD) Simulations. Classical electrodynamics simulations to resolve realistic field profiles in cavities, plasmonic resonators, and metasurfaces, bridging microscopic quantum-dynamics descriptions with experimentally relevant electromagnetic environments.
  • Nanophotonic and Plasmonic Field Structures. Use of simplified models and full-wave simulations to understand how local field enhancement, mode volumes, and spatial field distributions in plasmonic hotspots and nanophotonic resonators govern light–matter coupling strengths and resulting quantum dynamics.
  • Quantum-Classical Hybridization. Development of frameworks that couple FDTD-level electromagnetic descriptions to quantum-dynamical treatments of molecules and materials, enabling multiscale modelling of light-driven processes in realistic photonic device geometries.

Chirality and Spin-Selective Quantum Phenomena

An emerging direction in my research is the microscopic theory and quantum dynamical modeling of chiral light–matter interactions and chirality-induced spin selectivity (CISS). CISS—the experimental observation that chiral molecules preferentially transmit electrons of one spin polarization—remains incompletely understood from first principles, motivating rigorous theoretical investigation of its geometric and electromagnetic origins.

  • Chiral Light–Matter Interactions. Theoretical treatment of how chiral electromagnetic modes (circularly polarized light, chiral plasmonic near-fields) couple selectively to chiral molecular systems, with particular interest in enantioselective photophysics and the role of nanophotonic environments in amplifying chiral discrimination.
  • Chirality-Induced Spin Selectivity (CISS). Quantum dynamical modeling of spin-selective electron transport through chiral molecular assemblies, aiming to disentangle the relative roles of spin–orbit coupling, molecular geometry, and environmental decoherence in giving rise to the CISS effect.
  • Geometric and Electromagnetic Origins of Chiral Responses. Unified theoretical framework connecting molecular chirality, geometric phase effects, and structured electromagnetic environments to understand and predict chiral quantum phenomena across different scales and experimental platforms.
